Finite and infinite sets

There is an intimate connection between probability modeling in data science and the mathematics of sets. We distinguished

  • Finite sets: the elements can be mapped one-to-one to the set \(\{1, \ldots, n\}\) for a fixed and specified number \(n\).
  • Countably infinite sets: not finite, and elements can be mapped one-to-one to the natural numbers.
  • Uncountably infinite sets: not finite, but there is no one-to-one mapping to the natural numbers. The existence of such sets was demonstrated by Georg Cantor.

Discrete and continuous probability models

Discrete models

We have been informal in our treatment of random outcomes in chance situations, such as drawing a card from a well-shuffed deck. The notes from September 27 provide some formalism. We focused on the concepts of

  • defining the index sets for components of experiments (e.g., let \(i = 1, \ldots, 52\) denote the different card faces available, let \(j = 1, \ldots , 1000\) denote the number of draws)
  • clarifying assumptions of independence
  • recalling the form of the chi-squared statistic \[ \sum_{i=1}^K \frac{(O_i-E_i)^2}{E_i} \] where \(O_i\) and \(E_i\) are observed and expected counts for events of category \(i\). When the contributions to the observed counts are independent and all categories are equiprobable, this sum can be referred to the chi-squared distribution with \(K-1\) degrees of freedom.

We also used the murders dataset to form a contingency table and test the goodness of fit of a model prescribing equal risk of death by homicide across all states and D.C.

Continuous models

Measures like height and blood pressure are typically regarded as real numbers, and we use double-precision floating point numbers to compute on these. The fact that our computations are based on data values that are recorded with limited precision does not prevent us from using continuous probability models for such outcomes.

Exploring the CDC waterborne/food-related illness API response

library(httr)
z = GET("https://data.cdc.gov/api/v3/views/5xkq-dg7x/query.json")

We used zc = content(z) but then saved the result of that to waterborne.rda. Note that the API content was 29MB, but the compressed data in RDA format is 2.9MB in size.
